Tap and phase controls

Source: models/transformer.py and newton_raphson.py.

Control groups

Section titled “Control groups”

build_tap_controls creates TapControl objects. Each object has one scalar state, the shift scs_csc​, which is applied to every branch in the group:

V (LTC) DLIN with 0<Tmn<Tmx0<T_{mn}<T_{mx}0<Tmn​<Tmx​ and a controlled bus (DOPC CTAP) All LTCs regulating the same bus ak=ak0+sca_k = a_k^0 + s_cak​=ak0​+sc​ ∣Vc∣\lvert V_c\rvert∣Vc​∣

Parallel LTCs that regulate one bus share one shift, so they remain tap-equalized. After the network reduction, groups whose controlled buses merge are combined. Transformers that the reduction contracts leave the group.

The shift is bounded so that every branch respects its own limits:

max⁡k (Tmn,k−ak0)  ≤  sc  ≤  min⁡k (Tmx,k−ak0).\max_k\,(T_{mn,k} - a_k^0) \;\le\; s_c \;\le\; \min_k\,(T_{mx,k} - a_k^0).kmax​(Tmn,k​−ak0​)≤sc​≤kmin​(Tmx,k​−ak0​).

For phase shifters, the bounds come from DCTR.Fmin/Fmax in the Psh sign convention.

LTC voltage control with a dead band

Section titled “LTC voltage control with a dead band”

ANAREDE treats an LTC as a band controller with tolerance DCTE TLTC, which is 0.01 pu in the cases. The Newton solver follows the same logic:

  1. Every LTC group starts inactive: its tap is fixed.
  2. After an inner Newton solve, a group is activated when its controlled voltage leaves Vsp±TLTCV^{sp}\pm\text{TLTC}Vsp±TLTC. Here VspV^{sp}Vsp is the DBAR.V value of the controlled bus.
  3. An active group regulates to the nearest band edge:
gc(x)=∣Vc∣ κc−Vctarget=0,Vctarget=Vsp±TLTC.g_c(x) = \lvert V_c\rvert\,\kappa_c - V_c^{target} = 0,\qquad V_c^{target} = V^{sp} \pm \text{TLTC}.gc​(x)=∣Vc​∣κc​−Vctarget​=0,Vctarget​=Vsp±TLTC.

In this equation, κc\kappa_cκc​ is the voltage scale of the controlled bus in the reduced network.

Weak LTCs regulate the bus on their tap side, where the tap has little effect on that voltage. Without the dead band, they swing their taps across the whole range and distort downstream voltages by up to 0.12 pu. With the band, the solution matches ANAREDE to within 0.01 pu at the 99th percentile.

LTC groups are not activated in three cases:

  • the controlled bus is PV or the reference bus;
  • the controlled bus is regulated by an SVC;
  • the controlled bus does not exist.

Phase-shifter flow control

Section titled “Phase-shifter flow control”

A phase shifter holds the active power that leaves the Ext terminal and enters the branch:

gc(x)=Re⁡SExt(Vf,Vt,φk)−VspSbase=0.g_c(x) = \operatorname{Re} S_{\text{Ext}}(V_f, V_t, \varphi_k) - \frac{V_{sp}}{S_{base}} = 0 .gc​(x)=ReSExt​(Vf​,Vt​,φk​)−Sbase​Vsp​​=0.

It is active from the start.

Limits and conflicting controls

Section titled “Limits and conflicting controls”

Three safeguards in Newton keep the transformer controls well posed:

Safeguard Rule
Step limit If a Newton step asks for ∣Δs∣>0.05\lvert\Delta s\rvert > 0.05∣Δs∣>0.05 (tap) or >0.1> 0.1>0.1 rad (phase), the whole step is shortened. This keeps the search direction.
Limit When the shift is clamped at a bound and keeps pushing outward for 3 iterations, the group is fixed at that limit and its equation is dropped.
Conflict When a step asks for ∣Δs∣>0.2\lvert\Delta s\rvert > 0.2∣Δs∣>0.2 (tap) or >1> 1>1 rad (phase), the control has almost no independent effect on its target. It is fixed and the step is recomputed. Examples are two LTC groups regulating electrically identical buses, or an LTC whose target barely depends on its tap.

Reconciling settings with the stored voltages

Section titled “Reconciling settings with the stored voltages”

Saved cases contain the solved voltages, but some LTCs keep their pre-solution taps. Around the Sapeaçu 230/500 kV units at bus 6368 in CASO04, the stored tap 0.9695 across a 0.05 % reactance contradicts the stored voltages, which imply a tap of 0.918. The result is a 357 pu initial mismatch, and Newton diverges.

Before solving, reconcile_tap_controls corrects such inconsistencies. For every group, the terminal buses Tc\mathcal{T}_cTc​ of its branches are used to linearize the mismatch rrr with respect to the shift. The vector rrr contains P mismatches at non-reference terminals and Q mismatches at PQ terminals. The solver then takes one least-squares step:

g=∂r∂sc,sc←clip⁡(sc−g⊤rg⊤g,  scmin⁡,scmax⁡).g = \frac{\partial r}{\partial s_c},\qquad s_c \leftarrow \operatorname{clip}\Big(s_c - \frac{g^\top r}{g^\top g},\; s_c^{\min}, s_c^{\max}\Big).g=∂sc​∂r​,sc​←clip(sc​−g⊤gg⊤r​,scmin​,scmax​).

The correction is applied only when these conditions hold:

  • the terminal mismatch is significant, with ∥r∥2≥0.25\lVert r\rVert^2 \ge 0.25∥r∥2≥0.25;
  • the linearized mismatch drops to at most 25 % of its value.

Consistent transformers are therefore left unchanged. The solver performs up to three sweeps. Both solvers use this step; it is what makes the cold-start optimization possible.
